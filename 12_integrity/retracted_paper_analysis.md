# 2. The Violation

### Classification

This retracted paper can be classified into the following categories of research misconduct:

- **Undisclosed or Misused Artificial Intelligence**
- **Fabricated References**
- **Peer Review Manipulation**

Any references were not found on their repository and these simply did not exist, these references were generated by generative artificial intelligence to look for references scientific. According to the official retraction notice, the author acknowledged using generative Artificial Intelligence tools to locate and understand scientific references but failed to verify whether those references were authentic before submitting the manuscript.

The editorial investigation also identified evidence of manipulation of the peer-review process. Although there was no indication that experimental results or datasets had been fabricated, the presence of nonexistent references undermined the scientific reliability of the paper. Scientific literature depends on accurate citations that allow readers to verify previous work, reproduce arguments, and assess evidence. By including fabricated references, the article violated essential principles of transparency, accountability, and research integrity.

This case highlights an emerging ethical challenge associated with generative AI. Large Language Models (LLMs) can produce citations that appear convincing but correspond to publications that do not actually exist. Consequently, researchers remain fully responsible for verifying every citation, regardless of whether AI tools were used during manuscript preparation.

# 3. Detection

These references were identified by the editorial team of the *Journal of Medical Ethics* during a post-publication investigation.

Editors discovered that multiple references cited in the manuscript could not be located in recognized scientific databases. After contacting the author, it became clear that generative AI tools had been used to identify and summarize scientific literature. However, the author had not independently verified the existence or accuracy of those references before submission.

During the investigation, the journal also found evidence suggesting irregularities in the peer-review process, which further contributed to the decision to retract the article.

The paper was published in September 2025 and officially retracted in May 2026, approximately eight months later.

# 4. Systemic Analysis

This incident reflects broader systemic issues within modern scientific publishing rather than an isolated individual failure.

One significant factor is the increasing pressure placed on researchers to publish rapidly. The "publish or perish" culture encourages academics to use generative AI tools to accelerate literature reviews, manuscript drafting, and reference generation. While these technologies improve efficiency, they also increase the risk of introducing fabricated or inaccurate information when outputs are accepted without careful validation.

Another contributing factor is the current peer-review system. Peer reviewers primarily evaluate the novelty, methodology, and scientific contributions of a manuscript. They rarely verify every citation individually because doing so would require considerable additional time. As a result, fabricated references may remain unnoticed throughout the review process.

This case also exposes limitations in editorial workflows. Most journals currently rely on authors to ensure the accuracy of citations rather than employing automated systems capable of validating Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs) or detecting nonexistent publications. As generative AI becomes increasingly common, journals will likely need stronger editorial procedures and automated verification tools to reduce these risks.

Furthermore, many academic publishers are still developing policies governing the acceptable use of Artificial Intelligence. Although disclosure requirements have become more common, there remains uncertainty regarding how AI-generated content should be verified and how responsibility should be assigned when errors occur.

Overall, this retraction illustrates that maintaining research integrity requires shared responsibility among authors, reviewers, editors, publishers, and developers of AI technologies. Addressing only individual misconduct is insufficient without improving the broader scientific publication ecosystem.

# 5. Personal Transfer

After analyzing this retracted paper, I have identified one concrete research practice that I will apply throughout my doctoral studies.

> **I will manually verify every scientific reference before submitting any manuscript, conference paper, or thesis chapter.**

To achieve this, I will follow several best practices:

- Retrieve references only from trusted academic databases such as Scopus, Web of Science, IEE, Alicia, and Google Scholar.
- Verify that every DOI corresponds to an existing publication.
- Read each cited article to ensure that it genuinely supports the statements made in my research.
- Clearly disclose any use of Artificial Intelligence tools during manuscript preparation whenever required by journal policies.
- Maintain a reproducible research repository containing datasets, source code, documentation, and bibliography using version control systems such as Git.

As a Ph.D. student in Artificial Intelligence, I recognize that AI should function as an assistant rather than a substitute for scientific judgment. The responsibility for ensuring the accuracy, transparency, and credibility of published research always remains with the researcher. By implementing these practices, I can reduce the likelihood of introducing inaccurate information into my work and contribute to more trustworthy and reproducible scientific research.
