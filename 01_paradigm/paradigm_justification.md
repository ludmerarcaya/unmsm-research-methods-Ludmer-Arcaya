# Paradigm Justification

## Research Title

**Bayesian Artificial Intelligence on the Predictive Simulation of Uncertainty in Citizen Insecurity Scenarios in Lima Metropolitana, 2027**

## Research Topic and Context

Citizen insecurity in Peru has become one of the most critical social and technological challenges affecting urban sustainability, governance, and quality of life. This research focuses on the development of an Artificial Intelligence framework based on Bayesian Deep Learning and Generative AI to predict and simulate crime scenarios in Lima Metropolitana. The study is important because current security systems are mostly reactive and have limited predictive capabilities. The research also matters for Latin America because many countries in the region face similar public security problems and still have limited AI-based decision-support systems.

## Research Questions

How can Bayesian Deep Learning and Generative AI improve the prediction of citizen security incidents in Lima Metropolitana?

To what extent can AI-based simulation models support decision-making and reduce uncertainty in public security management in Peru?


## Research Paradigm and Justification

This research adopts a Design Science Research (DSR) and positivism paradigm because the objective of the study is to build and evaluate an AI artifact capable of solving a real-world problem related to citizen security using quantitative data to analyze, predict, simulations and statistical validation methods. The artifact itself represents the main scientific contribution of the research. A positivist approach is also relevant because the study will use quantitative data, predictive accuracy metrics, and statistical validation methods. Interpretivism was not selected because the research does not primarily focus on human perceptions or subjective experiences. The study instead emphasizes computational modeling, simulation, and measurable outcomes. Therefore, Design Science combined with positivism provides the best alignment with the goals of the project.


## Implications

The research will mainly use quantitative data such as crime statistics, geospatial information, demographic indicators, and public security datasets from Lima Metropolitana. The methodology will follow a Design Science process that includes problem identification, artifact development, model training, simulation, and evaluation. Bayesian Deep Learning models and Generative AI techniques will be used to predict and simulate crime scenarios under uncertain conditions. The evaluation phase will measure prediction accuracy, reliability, and practical usefulness for decision-making. This methodology is suitable because it supports iterative development, computational experimentation, and rigorous validation.


## One doubt or tensions

One important challenge is the quality and availability of crime data in Peru because some records may be incomplete or inconsistent. Another tension is the limited maturity level of AI adoption within public institutions responsible for citizen security. There are also ethical concerns regarding the responsible use of AI in sensitive social contexts such as crime prediction. Despite these limitations, the research has strong potential to contribute both scientifically and socially in Peru and Latin America.
